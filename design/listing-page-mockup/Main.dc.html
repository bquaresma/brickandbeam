The Story

Climb the narrow stair from the second floor and you're in a different house entirely.

The attic suite at 123 Butler Street keeps its 1910 bones — original sloped ceilings that dip toward deep dormer windows, wide-plank floors worn soft with a century of footsteps, and an exposed brick chimney that rises straight through the middle of the living room like it's holding the roof up itself. (It isn't. But it feels that way.)

This isn't a unit you'll find on a spec sheet. The primary bedroom tucks into the eaves — cozy, not cramped, with just enough headroom where it counts. A second, smaller room works as an office, nursery, or the best reading nook you'll ever own. Big enough to live in, small enough that every corner earns its keep.

Out the window: Butler Street's rooftops, and beyond them, the everyday hum of Lawrenceville — coffee roasters, a hardware store older than the house, and a ten-minute walk to just about everything.

The Layout

Not your standard one-bedroom.

The floor plan shows how the space is actually used — sloped ceilings along the roofline, an angled stair landing, and a second room that's smaller than code-minimum but works beautifully as an office or nursery. If you're counting on a rigid bed/bath grid to tell the whole story, this one won't fit it. That's the point.

Lawrenceville, Pittsburgh

One of Pittsburgh's most walkable neighborhoods — a stretch of Victorian rowhouses turned into coffee shops, record stores, and some of the city's best restaurants, all without losing its working-class bones. Butler Street is the spine: a ten-minute walk gets you three grocery options, a dozen restaurants, and the 54 bus line into downtown. This is a place where you'll actually use your feet.
